Actuator study

The animation follows the architecture in Benjamin Katz (2018), Figure 2-2: a motor, planetary transmission, bearings, and controller within one module. The thesis figure itself is not reproduced.

Mechanical geometry
Mini-Cheetah-Actuator by Saketh vegesna (SakethGG), using the published STEP parts. MIT License.
Controller geometry
moteus C1 by mjbots, via a public GoForFab KiCad export. Apache License 2.0. Component models follow the KiCad library license and generated-design exception.

This is a study using MIT-licensed Mini-Cheetah replica CAD and a representative moteus C1 controller. It is not an exact reconstruction of Katz’s hardware or electronics, or a dimensionally validated assembly for fabrication.

The source parts were converted from STEP, aligned, separated for the exploded view, and given steel-grey presentation materials. The live animation uses the original losslessly compressed mesh, preserving all 69 mechanical and controller parts and 973,838 triangles. Asset provenance and full attribution record the original files, revisions, and conversion details.

A recording of that same animation plays while the interactive renderer starts, then hands over at a matching pose. The recording manifest documents its full-resolution exports, near-lossless encoding, and quality checks.

Graph methodology

Each illustrative trajectory is normalized to its own 2026 value: index = original curve value ÷ original 2026 value × 100. The shared axis runs from 0 to 900; 100 is a reference year, not a maximum or a human-level threshold. The original curve timing and scenario bands are retained in the graph definitions.

The values describe the research design’s scenarios, not measured performance, prices, or fleet counts. Historical stories provide context and distinguish research demonstrations, launches, pilots, and operational reports. They are not inputs to a fitted numerical model, and a multiple of one line’s baseline is not a comparison of absolute capability across lines.

Every historical story links to its original publisher or research source. Language, humanoid, and hardware sources and data, specialist, and deployment sources retain dates, media references, and attribution. Original players and publisher-hosted video are used; no open-footage license is implied.

Future entries currently reserve space for Recraft storyboard sketches. Their scene descriptions explore possible developments; they do not document events. The sketch frames remain intentionally empty until the illustrations are ready.
