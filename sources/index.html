Actuator study

The animation follows the architecture in Benjamin Katz (2018), Figure 2-2: a motor, planetary transmission, bearings, and controller within one module. The thesis figure itself is not reproduced.

Mechanical geometry
Mini-Cheetah-Actuator by Saketh vegesna (SakethGG), using the published STEP parts. MIT License.
Controller geometry
moteus C1 by mjbots, via a public GoForFab KiCad export. Apache License 2.0. Component models follow the KiCad library license and generated-design exception.

This is a study using MIT-licensed Mini-Cheetah replica CAD and a representative moteus C1 controller. It is not an exact reconstruction of Katz’s hardware or electronics, or a dimensionally validated assembly for fabrication.

The source parts were converted from STEP, aligned, separated for the exploded view, and given steel-grey presentation materials. The live animation uses the original losslessly compressed mesh, preserving all 69 mechanical and controller parts and 973,838 triangles. Asset provenance and full attribution record the original files, revisions, and conversion details.

A recording of that same animation plays while the interactive renderer starts, then hands over at a matching pose. The recording manifest documents its full-resolution exports, near-lossless encoding, and quality checks.

Graph methodology

Every line uses its own reported 2025 baseline: index = 100 × source value ÷ 2025 source value. The shared compressed index scale keeps changes around that baseline visible alongside much larger AI projections. The same transformation applies to every line: sign(ln(index / 100)) × ln(1 + |ln(index / 100)|), implemented with D3’s symmetric logarithmic scale. Axis labels and readouts show normalized indices. Equal vertical distances do not imply equal additive or proportional changes, and gaps between separately normalized deployment lines do not represent count shares.

Published observations retain their original values. Five historical paths extend to 2015 through reported values and labeled reconstruction; humanoid deployment begins in 2025, with earlier counts unavailable. Interpolated or backward-extrapolated history is modeled context, including hypothetical values before a benchmark or corpus existed. Curves use SciPy’s shape-preserving cubic interpolation through annual values without overshooting adjacent anchors. Markers identify reported observations; dashed futures identify projections. The plot remains continuous and densely sampled, while inspection steps are annual from 2015 through 2026, then 2030, 2035, and 2040. Readouts retain raw units, dates, and partial-year coverage.

LLM performance
METR task-completion horizon at 80% success: the estimated human task duration, in minutes, associated with 80% model success on the evaluated tasks. The 2025 baseline is 66.002649 minutes. The published results retain confidence intervals and evaluation versions; METR’s documented series combines earlier TH1.0 evaluations with TH1.1 results from 2023 onward. These are principally software-task evaluations, not a universal intelligence scale.
Humanoid deployment
Counterpoint reports approximately 16,000 additional installations worldwide in 2025, the only full-year historical anchor. Its 2026 update reports over 22,000 shipments in H1 and forecasts more than 50,000 for the full year. The scenario uses 50,000 as a conservative anchor; neither figure is an observed full-year 2026 count. These annual flows include research, data collection, entertainment, service, and industrial uses. Shipments proxy deployment, not verified productive operation; no pre-2025 counts are invented.
Specialist robots
Worldwide annual industrial robot installations from the International Federation of Robotics. This is an industrial-deployment proxy for specialist robots. The 2026 report records 603,307 installations in 2025. Earlier observations retain the rounding precision of the published tables and charts.
Robot deployment
Worldwide annual industrial robot installations, also from IFR: 603,307 in 2025, with a forecast of 655,000 for 2026. The historical charts and executive summaries supply the same industrial-installation history used by Specialist robots. This overlap is intentional. Both deployment readouts now use robots/year, but IFR’s industrial population differs from Counterpoint’s humanoid population; neither supplies a census of all robots or an observed humanoid market share.
Interaction data
Released real-robot training episodes in three named corpus families: RoboNet (162,417), DROID’s initial RLDS release (92,233), and AgiBot Beta (1,003,672). The 2025 inventory totals 1,258,322. Beta replaces its 92,214-episode Alpha subset; the two releases are not added together. RoboNet’s exact count comes from its pinned TFDS representation. This is release accounting for selected public corpora, not a census of all private data or equally long physical interactions. Aggregate mixtures, alternate versions, relabeled copies, and simulated episodes are excluded.
Hardware cost
The Bank of Japan’s Robots producer-price index excluding consumption tax, series PRCG20_1201350029, from the official monthly data. Complete-year values average the twelve monthly observations; the 2025 mean is 105.708333. A partial 2026 average is marked year to date. The price-index methodology covers Japanese domestic robot goods. Lower values mean lower prices; this is not a dollars-per-actuator series.

Future assumptions. The LLM scenario uses logistic growth calibrated to METR’s record-setting TH1.1 80% horizons, then holds its 2035 level through 2040. Its ten-work-year scale anchor, with 2,000 hours per work year, is an author-selected ceiling informed by the autonomous-coding threshold in AI 2040’s takeoff model. The slowdown follows Plan A’s scaling strategy. This is a scenario inspired by that work, not a published METR forecast or an empirical ceiling.

The authored total robot-deployment scenario starts at 655,000 robots/year in 2026 and follows the LLM path’s proportional growth and 2035 pause: R(t) = 655000 × L(t) / L(2026). It reaches about 4.23 billion robots/year by 2040. This extrapolation uses industrial installations as its starting proxy; it is not an IFR forecast or a measured all-robot census. Humanoid deployments initially track this growth and then rise sublinearly. With g = R(t) / 655000, the authored rule is H(t) = 50000 × [1 + (10 / 0.6) × ((1 + (g - 1) / 10)^0.6 - 1)]. Counts do not decrease, while their ratio to the modeled total falls from about 7.63% in 2026 to 0.94% in 2040. The projected humanoid panel reports this modeled fraction; it is not observed market share.

The public-data scenario approaches a plateau 30 times the latest inventory, reaching 95% of it in 2031. Specialist installations start with IFR’s 2026 forecast of 655,000, reach four times their 2025 level in 2031, then decline to one quarter in 2040. Hardware costs approach a 3% floor with a 1.1-year halving time for the remaining distance above it, falling by approximately 93% from the 2026 anchor by 2031. These targets are author-selected scenario assumptions. Deterministic, monotone PCHIP timing variations provide gentle changes of pace while fixing the 2026, 2030, 2031, 2035, and 2040 anchors. They introduce no additional observations; smooth connectors preserve the resulting annual model values.

The observations and provenance retain source values, dates, units, precision, and baseline definitions. The calculation model reproduces normalization and projections from the audited source inputs. The earlier HumanoidBench scores and IFR operating-stock data remain archived there and in the retired baseline fixtures; they are not relabeled as deployment counts. Reported confidence intervals use the same fixed 2025 denominator and describe source-estimate uncertainty, not confidence in future scenarios.

Videos provide event context for the selected year; the separate metric citation supplies the numerical evidence. Language, humanoid, and hardware event sources and data, specialist, and deployment event sources retain media attribution. Future storyboard descriptions remain scenarios, with empty frames reserved for the Recraft sketches.
